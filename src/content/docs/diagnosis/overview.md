## Clinical question

How can suspected cardiac amyloidosis be evaluated while distinguishing transthyretin amyloidosis from light-chain amyloidosis and other causes of increased wall thickness?

## Practical framework

1. **Recognize the phenotype and clinical context.** Consider heart-failure phenotype, unexplained increased ventricular wall thickness, conduction disease and relevant extracardiac findings.
2. **Exclude a monoclonal protein process.** Serum and urine immunofixation plus serum free light chains are used to evaluate for AL amyloidosis.
3. **Use cardiac imaging appropriately.** Echocardiography and CMR characterize the phenotype; bone-avid tracer scintigraphy, including 99mTc-PYP, can support non-biopsy diagnosis of ATTR-CM in the appropriate clinical setting.
4. **Escalate when results are discordant.** Tissue diagnosis and amyloid typing may be required when monoclonal protein testing is abnormal, imaging is equivocal, or multiple diagnoses remain plausible.
5. **Perform TTR genetic testing after ATTR is established** to distinguish hereditary from wild-type disease.

## Evidence note

Diagnostic thresholds and exact testing sequences should be referenced to the current guideline or consensus statement used by the treating institution.
